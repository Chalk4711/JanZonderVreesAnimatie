# CINEMATOGRAPHY BIBLE

Status: Canon v2

## Philosophy

The camera is a storyteller, not a recorder.

Every scene must have a dominant emotional camera intention.

## Core Influences

- Blue Eye Samurai: controlled composition, mature staging, graphic silhouettes.
- Arcane: intimate emotional framing and expressive faces.
- The Last of Us: restrained camera, grounded tension and human proximity.
- Klaus: warmth and painterly staging for home and memory.

## Core Rules

- Prefer motivated camera movement.
- Avoid random motion.
- Use scale to communicate power.
- Use intimacy to communicate emotional truth.
- Use stillness when a character is emotionally trapped or deeply present.
- Use reveals to let the audience discover the world with Jan.

## Emotional Camera Language

Safety = stable framing.

Danger = dynamic framing, obstructed views, partial silhouettes.

Wonder = slow reveal, widening frame, layered depth.

Belonging = warm close framing and shared physical space.

Authority = low angles, symmetry, stone backgrounds, distance.

Mystery = foreground obstruction, mist, shadows, delayed reveal.

Transformation = backlight, thresholds, slow push-in or pull-back.

## Character Camera Language

### Jan

Jan's camera is restless early in the story.

- more movement;
- more handheld feeling;
- impulsive reframing;
- low-angle aspiration shots;
- sudden reveals.

As Jan matures, the camera around him becomes more deliberate and stable.

### Hilda

Hilda's camera observes before it moves.

- careful framing;
- over-the-shoulder observation;
- reaction shots before action;
- visual emphasis on what she notices.

Hilda scenes should often allow the viewer to understand the room through her intelligence.

### Neeltje

Neeltje's camera stays.

- stable closeups;
- warm interiors;
- hands, face, work objects;
- slow emotional framing.

The camera should make the audience feel the gravitational pull of home.

### Dokus

Dokus's camera often follows.

- trailing movement;
- glances toward Jan;
- framing that shows belonging or exclusion;
- gradual shift toward independent framing as his arc develops.

### Stansen

Stansen's camera grounds.

- strong horizontal lines;
- weight;
- practical distance;
- stable work-based framing.

## Opening Sequence Camera Canon

The opening should move from world to person:

Scheldt -> Harbor -> Fish Market -> Krabbenstraatje -> Fish Shop -> Plaice Sign -> Attic Window -> Jan.

This sequence should feel dreamlike but grounded.

The camera should not rush to Jan. It should first teach the viewer that Jan belongs to a place, a community and a household.

## Folklore Reveal Rule

Never reveal the supernatural too quickly.

Preferred order:

1. sound;
2. reaction;
3. shadow;
4. detail;
5. partial form;
6. full reveal only when emotionally earned.

## Signature Shots

- Scheldt dawn reveal.
- Harbor masts against pale sky.
- Fish market life in layered depth.
- Neeltje and Jan in warm shop light.
- Jan in attic window looking outward.
- Hilda noticing danger before others.
- Forest threshold shot.
- Low-angle civic authority shot.
- Wide Flemish landscape with small human figures.

## Shot Composition

Frames should use:

- layered foregrounds;
- architectural framing;
- windows and doorways;
- ropes, nets and beams;
- candlelight and mist;
- silhouettes against sky or firelight.

## Production Rule

Every storyboard panel should indicate the emotional function of the shot, not only the camera angle.
