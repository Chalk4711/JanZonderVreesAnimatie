# PROJECT MASTER CONTEXT

Version: 2.0
Status: Canon Source for AI/Codex

## 1. Executive Summary

**Jan Zonder Vrees** is being developed as a premium animated series inspired by the Flemish legend of Jan zonder Vrees. The project should not be approached as a simple YouTube animation or a generic medieval adventure. The ambition is closer to a streaming-quality animated series: emotionally grounded, visually cinematic, historically plausible, and suitable for a broad modern audience.

The heart of the project is not spectacle. The heart is a coming-of-age story about courage, belonging, family, responsibility and letting go. Jan begins as a fearless, impulsive boy from Antwerp's harbor world. Over the season he must learn that true courage is not the absence of fear, but acting responsibly despite fear.

## 2. Core Identity

The series is best understood as:

> A coming-of-age epic set against a warm, living, historically inspired Flemish medieval world.

The world matters deeply, but the world exists to support character and emotion. Antwerp, the Scheldt, the harbor, the fish market, the Krabbenstraatje, Neeltje's fish shop, Stansen's farm and the Free Forest are not just locations. They are emotional zones.

## 3. Quality Ambition

The target is **premium streaming animation quality**. The project references:

- **Blue Eye Samurai** for mature cinematography, visual restraint, atmospheric depth and historical stylization.
- **Klaus** for warmth, humanity, painterly light and emotional accessibility.
- **Arcane** for visual hierarchy, strong character drama and production polish.
- **Wolfwalkers** for contrasting visual worlds such as city versus forest.

The project must learn from these works, not imitate them.

## 4. Creative North Star

Jan Zonder Vrees should feel like:

> A living Flemish painting, filmed with the cinematic discipline of Blue Eye Samurai and Arcane, but warmed by the humanity and emotional light of Klaus.

The viewer should not think: "This is historically exact."

The viewer should think: "This feels as if medieval Flanders could really have lived like this. I want to return to this world."

## 5. Adaptation Philosophy

This is not a museum reconstruction. It is also not generic fantasy.

The chosen balance is:

- historical plausibility as foundation;
- cinematic readability as design method;
- emotion and character as final authority.

Historical details are important, but they must not damage story, character, emotion or visual clarity. If a choice is historically perfect but emotionally weak, controlled stylization is allowed. If a choice looks beautiful but breaks the Flemish historical atmosphere, it must be rejected.

## 6. Major Themes

### Courage
Courage is not fearlessness. Courage is responsible action despite fear.

### Belonging
Jan does not only seek adventure. He seeks his place in the world.

### Growing Up
The story follows the transition from impulsive freedom to mature responsibility.

### Family
Family is not only blood. It includes Neeltje, Dokus, Hilda, the neighborhood and the community.

### Letting Go
This is the hidden deep theme. Neeltje must let Jan grow. Jan must let go of childhood. Hilda must let go of total control. Dokus must let go of fear.

## 7. Emotional Core

The audience should not only admire Jan. They should care about him, understand him, worry about him, and want him to grow.

The final emotional goal of season 1 is not simply: "That was exciting."

The goal is:

> I want to return to this world and these people.

## 8. Visual Strategy

The project uses a painterly cinematic animation style. The strongest current production direction is a hybrid approach: 3D or spatially consistent staging with painterly 2D-style texture, lighting and composition.

Do not default to full fantasy, Disney proportions, or generic medieval European designs.

Core visual rules:

- historically plausible Flemish world around Antwerp circa 1400;
- strong silhouettes;
- readable characters;
- rich atmosphere;
- believable materials: wool, linen, wood, rope, stone, wet market surfaces;
- emotional color logic;
- no modern elements;
- no German fairy-tale village look as dominant style;
- no generic high fantasy design.

## 9. Cinematography Strategy

The camera is a storyteller, not a recorder.

The series should use mature cinematic staging. Establish world before character when appropriate. Let atmosphere and camera movement introduce emotional meaning.

Important camera principles:

- world first, then community, then character;
- stable framing for safety and home;
- dynamic tracking for Jan's impulsiveness;
- observing frames for Hilda;
- lower and admiring perspectives for Dokus toward Jan;
- warm close framing for Neeltje;
- obstructed, misty, uncertain framing for folklore and fear.

The opening "dream camera" route is canonically important:

Scheldt → Harbor → Fish Market → Krabbenstraatje → Fish Shop → Plaice Sign → Attic Window → Jan.

## 10. Sound Strategy

Sound and music are emotional storytelling instruments. The project should not use generic epic fantasy music as default.

The sonic world should combine:

- historically inspired Flemish / Western medieval colors;
- fiddle, flute, lute-like plucked strings, drones, simple percussion;
- modern cinematic warmth;
- environmental sounds integrated into rhythm and music.

Antwerp must be heard: harbor bells, gulls, rope, water, market calls, wooden carts, footsteps on wet stone.

Silence is an important tool.

## 11. Character Strategy

Character consistency is more important than style experimentation. A beautiful image that breaks identity is not acceptable.

Character Lock is mandatory before large-scale production.

Character Lock sequence:

1. Canon Candidate
2. Hero Portrait
3. Production Turnaround
4. Expression Sheet
5. Pose Sheet
6. Costume Breakdown
7. Prop Sheet
8. Production Lock

The most important characters are Jan, Hilda and Neeltje. Neeltje currently has an approved Canon Candidate v1 in Notion's Character Asset Library.

## 12. Production Map Strategy

The world must be spatially coherent. Key places:

- Scheldt
- Harbor
- Fish Market
- Krabbenstraatje
- Neeltje's Fish Shop
- Grote Markt
- Cemetery
- City Gate
- Stansen Farm
- Free Forest

The production map must support storyboarding, AI generation, camera continuity and world consistency.

## 13. Symbolic Motifs

Recurring motifs should gather emotional meaning over time:

- Scheldt: change, destiny, larger world;
- gulls: freedom, harbor life;
- dried plaice sign: home, Neeltje, origin;
- ropes and masts: connection, fate, trade, responsibility;
- mist: hidden truth, folklore, uncertainty;
- bells: time, duty, community;
- windows: longing and perspective;
- thresholds: choice, growth, transition;
- light in darkness: hope, home, humanity.
